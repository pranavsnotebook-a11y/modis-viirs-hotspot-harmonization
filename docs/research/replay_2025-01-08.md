# Replay: the 500 kV line trip of 8 January 2025

One real event, run through the same code as the DOWNWIND page. One event is an illustration, not a validation.

## The event

On Wednesday 8 January 2025 at 17:01 Paraguay time (20:01 UTC) a 500 kV transmission line feeding the Asunción
area tripped. ANDE, the utility, said the cause was smoke from a fire near the line, not under it: the wind carried
the smoke between the conductors and the protection isolated the line. Press reports name the Ayolas to Villa Hayes
line and place the fire near Vapor Cué National Park (Caraguatay, Cordillera).

- Última Hora, 10 January 2025: https://www.ultimahora.com/humareda-causo-corte-de-ande-y-no-se-descarta-que-vuelva-a-ocurrir
- El Prisma, 9 January 2025: https://elprisma.com.py/un-incendio-forestal-produjo-el-apagon-de-ayer/

## Questions, fixed before running

1. Did the satellites record a fire near a 500 kV line before the trip?
2. Does the FIRELAB plume, with the reanalysis wind for those hours, carry smoke across a 500 kV line?

## Data

| Input | Source | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Fires | FIRMS country archive 2025, standard processing: MODIS C6.1, VIIRS S-NPP, VIIRS NOAA-20 | observed |
| Lines | OpenStreetMap ways 585106818 and 807985047, `power=line`, 500 kV | mapped |
| Wind | ERA5 reanalysis via the Open-Meteo archive API at 25.23°S, 56.79°W, hourly | modelled |
| Smoke | FIRELAB Gaussian plume, savanna and grassland emission factor, fire held at observed FRP | scenario |

NOAA-21 is not in the keyless archive, so its passes are missing. The case file is `replay_2025-01-08/case.json`.

## Result

Run with `node scripts/replay_line_trip.mjs`. All rows are in `replay_2025-01-08/result.json`.

**1. Yes, the satellites saw fires first.** There are 30 fire clusters within 30 km of the park in the 12 hours
before the trip. The last passes were S-NPP at 17:05 UTC, NOAA-20 at 17:28 UTC and Aqua at 18:08 UTC, between 1.9
and 2.9 hours before the trip. Fires at the same two spots near the park were also recorded on 7 January at 17:24
and 17:47 UTC, a day earlier.

**2. Yes, the modelled smoke crosses a 500 kV line, in two places.**

| Line (by mapped endpoints) | Fire | FRP, per pass | Fire to line | Modelled crossing | PM2.5 added at the line |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Itaipú to Villa Hayes, way 585106818, 1 km from the park | 25.265°S, 56.765°W, 3 passes | 6 to 16 MW | 4.8 to 5.1 km, fire south of line | 19 to 46 min after leaving the fire | 3 to 9 µg/m³ |
| Ayolas to Villa Hayes, way 807985047, 19 km west of the park | 25.215°S, 57.069°W, 2 passes | 16 to 29 MW | 3.1 to 3.4 km, fire south-west of line | 13 to 33 min | 6 to 22 µg/m³ |

The wind was from the south (186° to 197°) at 1.9 to 4.3 m/s at 100 m. Turning it 30° either way keeps a crossing on
both lines.

**A distance ring gets the order wrong.** The three detections closest to a line, 1.6 to 1.7 km north of the line by
the park, are not downwind: the southerly wind carries their smoke away from it. A ring would rank them first and
the upwind fire 5 km away below them.

## What this does not show

- **Not which fire caused the trip.** Two candidate fires sit upwind of two different lines. The press names the
  Ayolas line and a fire near Vapor Cué; in OpenStreetMap the line 1 km from the park runs to Itaipú, and the Ayolas
  line is 19 km to the west. The press account and the map do not agree, and this replay cannot settle it.
- **Not a fault prediction.** The scenario puts a few µg/m³ of PM2.5 at ground level at the line. A flashover needs
  dense smoke and hot gas between conductors tens of metres up. The model only shows that the line was downwind.
- **Light wind.** ERA5 has a 0.25° grid and the wind was 2 to 4 m/s. Direction is uncertain at that speed.
- **Fire held constant.** FRP is from the pass, two to three hours before the trip. The fire may have grown or moved.
- **Lead time.** Public FIRMS data arrives up to three hours after a pass. The 17:05 to 18:08 UTC detections could
  have reached a control room only around the 20:01 UTC trip. The 7 January detections would have arrived a day
  ahead, as a fire persisting near a line.

## Would the 10-minute satellite have helped?

Checked in `replay_2025-01-08_goes.md`. On 8 January GOES-16 flagged neither candidate fire in any of the 60 scans
before the trip, and cloud was not the reason: the pixels were processed and found to hold no fire. It did flag the
western fire on the evening of 7 January, for six consecutive scans from 21:20 UTC (27 to 68 MW), about 22 hours
before the trip if the data arrives 30 minutes after the scan. So the faster satellite gives no minutes-ahead
warning for this event either. The usable warning, from both kinds of satellite, is the day before: a fire
persisting near a line.

## How often would a distance rule fire?

Counted from the same 2025 archive (MODIS, S-NPP, NOAA-20; type 0, presumed vegetation fire) against all mapped
500 kV ways in Paraguay, about 657 km of line:

| Fire within | Detections in 2025 | Days with at least one |
|---|---|---|
| 2 km of a 500 kV line | 1,593 | 196 |
| 5 km | 4,406 | 245 |
| 10 km | 8,708 | 281 |

A plain distance rule would raise a flag on two days out of three.

**The wind test does not cut the number of flagged days.** Measured for all of 2025 in `alert_load_2025.md`: the
wind rule flags 257 days against 244 for the 5 km ring, because it also reaches fires up to 25 km away. What it
changes is which fires are flagged: of the 2,422 fire clusters inside a 5 km ring, 1,215 (50%) are upwind of the
line, and 61% of the wind rule's flags come from fires a 5 km ring misses. So the wind test is a way to rank and
sort a daily list, not a way to make the list rare. Scoring either rule needs ANDE's own trip log, which we do not
have.
