Method and limits

What this page does. It takes each fire the satellites recorded, runs the FIRELAB smoke scenario with the modelled wind for that hour, and checks which mapped assets the smoke path reaches. A distance ring around an asset cannot tell upwind from downwind; this can.

OBSERVED Fires. NASA FIRMS detections from MODIS and VIIRS, taken from the harmonized layer so that a fire seen by both sensors in one pass is used once. Pixels of one satellite pass within 1.5 km and 10 minutes are grouped and their fire radiative power is summed, assuming one fire. The same fire seen on a later pass is listed once per asset, with the number of passes.

MAPPED Assets. OpenStreetMap: power lines, roads, hospitals, schools. Volunteer data: it can be incomplete or mislabelled and it is not the operator's record.

Times. Times in bulletins use the time zone of the selected region. Brazil has several time zones and the bulletin uses Brasília time.

MODELLED Wind. Open-Meteo weather models at 100 m, on a 0.25° grid, for the hour of the satellite pass and the two hours after it. In light wind (under about 3 m/s) the modelled direction is unreliable; such rows are marked. Wind is requested for at most 240 such cells per computation, the strongest fires first; when smaller fires are left out the page says how many, and none of their assets is called "not downwind" because those fires were not assessed.

SCENARIO Smoke path. The FIRELAB Gaussian plume (Briggs dispersion, Sofiev plume rise, Akagi emission factor for savanna and grassland), with the fire held at its observed power for three hours. Fire clusters whose summed power is below the chosen minimum (5 MW unless you change it) are left out and counted on the page, because at night the formula gives even small, distant fires a narrow path that is weak evidence. A line is listed as crossed when the plume axis passes over it and the modelled ground-level PM2.5 added along it reaches 1 µg/m³. A point is listed as downwind at the same level.

FAST · COARSE Fast look. A second, coarser source: INPE Programa Queimadas publishes a small file every 10 minutes with GOES-19 detections, South America only (about 2 km pixels). FIRMS reaches the public up to 3 hours after a pass; in one measurement of 543 files, GOES-19 detections were a median of 20 minutes before the file time stamp. These files carry no fire power and no confidence value, so no plume is run and no smoke amount is shown: a row means only that the pixel is upwind of the asset by the modelled wind, within 10 km. One scan is weak evidence and several consecutive scans are stronger; rows are ranked by the number of 10-minute scans, then distance. There are more false alarms than with MODIS or VIIRS, small or short fires can be missed, and cloud hides fires. If an asset lies within 2 km of the pixel centre the wind test cannot be trusted. The project has not validated this source against ground reports. If INPE cannot be reached the page says so and the other tabs keep working.

Bulletin languages. English, Spanish and Portuguese. The Spanish and Portuguese texts are fixed templates, not yet reviewed by a native speaker; the short form has at most 160 characters and uses only the GSM 7-bit alphabet.

What it does not do. It does not predict a line fault, visibility on a road, or anyone's health. It does not see fires between satellite passes, under cloud, or too small for the sensor. FIRMS data reaches the public up to three hours after the pass, so this is a watch list for the hours after a pass, not a first alarm. Not an operational alert service.

Next look. Estimated from the times of the passes in the last days of data that recorded at least one fire in the region. It is not an orbit prediction.
