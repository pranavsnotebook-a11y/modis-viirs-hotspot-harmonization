Method, sources and limits
A scenario model, not a forecast
    This is a teaching and exploration tool. The plume is a scenario from a single satellite snapshot and published formulas. It has not been validated against measurements, and it must not be used for health or evacuation decisions. Use official air-quality services for those.

    What the animation shows

    • Wind streaks move in the direction of the one modelled (or your) wind at the fire, toward wind-from + 180°, with speed in proportion to the wind. It is a uniform overpass wind drawn inside the 200 km model domain, not a wind field.
    • Smoke drifts downwind and is drawn only over the grid cells the model computed, more opaque in higher concentration bands. Time is sped up; puffs are a visual texture, not particles the model tracks.
    • Play re-runs the same steady-state model for 1, 2, … 24 hours with the weather held fixed. Each step is a separate computed result; nothing between the steps is invented.
    Welcome to FIRELAB
    Five steps, about a minute
    1. Pick a fire. Choose a region, then click any orange dot on the map or pick one from the list. Every dot is a real NASA FIRMS detection.
    2. Watch the wind. The moving streaks show the modelled wind at the satellite pass, which decides where the smoke goes.
    3. Change the scenario. Drag the hours, change the vegetation, or try the worst case. Press ▶ Play to step the plume hour by hour.
    4. Read the results. The results panel updates with the map. Pin a run as A to compare it with the next.
    5. Know what it is. Orange SCENARIO values are model estimates, not measurements. “Scenario model · not a forecast” opens the method.
