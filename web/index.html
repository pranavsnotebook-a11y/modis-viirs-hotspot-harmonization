Spatial criterion

Footprint overlap

Each detection’s real pixel rectangle (FIRMS scan × track, in km) must overlap, so resolution and swath-edge growth are respected rather than one fixed radius.

Temporal criterion

Time window

Acquisition times, in UTC, must fall within the selected window. Among overlapping MODIS pixels, the closest in time wins, then the nearest.

Quality control

Keep provenance

Matched exports keep both sets of source IDs, each sensor’s FRP and confidence, the time difference, centre distance, rival candidates, match strength and the window used. No source record is altered or deleted.

Strength · strong

Same pass, core hit

Time gap ≤ 30 min, the VIIRS centre inside the core of the MODIS pixel, and no rival MODIS pixel within 15 min. Both sensors very likely saw the same fire at almost the same moment.

Strength · moderate

Close, one doubt

Time gap ≤ 30 min with an edge overlap or a rival, or ≤ 120 min with a core hit and no rival. Probably the same burning area, seen up to two hours apart.

Strength · weak / ambiguous

Same place, different moment

Every other pair inside the window: usually a different overpass of a fire that may have grown, moved or ended. Ambiguous means a second MODIS pixel qualifies almost as well, so the group is a rule choice.

Limitations
  • MODIS and VIIRS satellites rarely pass together. Most matches join different overpasses, hours apart, of a fire that may have changed.
  • “VIIRS only” does not mean MODIS could not see it: overpass time and cloud also decide what each satellite records.
  • Records are pixels, not fires. One fire can yield many records, and repeat passes are kept as separate observations.
  • FRP is not corrected between sensors. Our test on 25 near-coincident pairs did not beat leaving it unchanged, so both values are shown side by side.
  • Footprint orientation is estimated from the orbit because FIRMS provides none.
  • Aqua has drifted to a later afternoon pass, so it now trails NOAA-20 and NOAA-21 by one to three hours. Very few pairs are within 30 minutes, so most matches are “weak” or “moderate” by design.
  • When a satellite delivered no data (for example S-NPP in an outage), its pixels are absent, not zero, and its fires cannot be matched.
